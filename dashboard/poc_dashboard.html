Roadmap

Where the POC stands, and what we need from you

Decisions

  • Choose one pilot area. The reservoir data covers Chonburi; the network model covers Rayong. One area needs both.
  • Confirm nodes 71 and 678. They carry 100,000 m³/day each and decide whether the NPL3 zone is short of capacity.
  • Confirm the TM Pond pumps. Pumps 3 to 6 cannot run in the model. Are they standby or out of service?
  • Agree the lowest tank levels. Holding NPL2_HT and NPL3_HT lower saves about 7.5% of pumping energy in the model, but keeps less water in reserve.
  • Agree the KPIs. Cost per m³, forecast error and planning time, measured before and after.

Data

  • Customer meter readings for the 28 network customers: daily for 3 years and hourly for 3 months.
  • SCADA records: pump status, flow and power, tank levels and key pressures.
  • Electricity tariff and pump power data to replace the assumed time-of-use prices, plus efficiency tests for the NPL3 and TM Pond pumps.
  • Allocation and contract changes ahead of time; they drive demand more than weather does.

How these numbers were produced. Reservoir figures come from the Chonburi irrigation daily water report (sheet WB บางพระ, 1990 to ), cleaned with every change logged. Network figures come from EPANET runs of Demand-2023 v20RY to MTP new TM2P.inp. Forecasts and pump plans were built in MATLAB R2026b.

  • Electricity cost uses an assumed weekday time-of-use tariff (); replace it with the actual tariff before quoting costs.
  • The network model has no daily demand pattern, so every hour carries the same demand.
  • The outlook treats each past year's inflow as equally likely and assumes diversions follow the last three years' pattern.
